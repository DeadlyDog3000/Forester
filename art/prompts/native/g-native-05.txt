A late 17th-century oil portrait on canvas, in the manner of John Verelst's 1710 portraits of Haudenosaunee leaders: half-length, three-quarter view, a dark brown background, soft warm light from the left, visible brushwork and fine craquelure, a dignified and respectful likeness of a real person. A Wabanaki war leader, older, grey in the scalp lock: a beaver-fur mantle, a wampum collar of white and purple shell beads, a calm and weathered face, holding a trade musket across his body. No text, no frame, no signature. Square.
