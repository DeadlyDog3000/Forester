A late 17th-century oil portrait on canvas, in the manner of John Verelst's 1710 portraits of Haudenosaunee leaders: half-length, three-quarter view, a dark brown background, soft warm light from the left, visible brushwork and fine craquelure, a dignified and respectful likeness of a real person. A Muscogee war leader in his forties: a roach of red deer hair, painted black stripes on the chin, a breastplate of shell beads, a finger-woven sash over one shoulder, holding a tomahawk with a carved wooden handle. No text, no frame, no signature. Square.
